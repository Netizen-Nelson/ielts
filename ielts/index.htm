As a Taipei citizen, you know that feeling on a packed train when you spot an empty seat by the door — like a gift from the universe. The secret is reading the signs around you. Watch the person with the big shopping bags — they're usually the first to get off. Or the woman in the work outfit checking her phone — probably getting off before the transfer station. The moment between stations is when everything happens. People near the exit start shifting. The guy with headphones suddenly pulls them off. That's your cue — the seat is about to open up. Here's the thing about seats on the priority row — many people avoid them out of guilt. So if you don't mind a little awkward eye contact, that's your best shot during peak hours. It sounds calculating, but it's basically just a life skill at this point. 身為台北市民，你一定體驗過在擁擠的地鐵裡發現門口空位的感覺——簡直就像老天爺送的禮物。 秘訣在於觀察周遭的動靜。注意那些拎著大購物袋的人——他們通常是最先下車的。或者觀察那些穿著職業裝、低頭看手機的女士——他們很可能在換乘站之前就下車了。 換乘站之間是關鍵時刻。靠近出口的人開始挪動位置。戴著耳機的人突然摘下耳機。這就是你該挪動的訊號──空位來了。 關於優先排的座位，很多人會因為不好意思而避開它們。所以，如果你不介意偶爾尷尬的眼神交流，那麼在高峰時段，優先排的座位就是你的最佳選擇。 這聽起來像是在算計，但基本上，這只不過是一種生活技能罷了。
